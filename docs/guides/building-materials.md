# Building Materials

What a building is made of, from the studs out and from the floor up: wall materials, glass, the
structure and framing behind them, and the finishes that go on last. It's the **CSM: Building
Materials** module, across three creative tabs: **Building Materials**, **Structure & Framing** and
**Interior Finishes**.

The doors and garage doors in the Building Materials tab are covered in [Doors](doors.md), and its
chain-link fences, with the rest of the Construction Site tab, in
[Construction Site](construction-site.md#fences).

## Wall materials

Every material here is a **set**: a full block, stairs, a slab and a fence, all with the same
face, so a wall, its coping, a step and a low fence can be built of one material.

| Material | Blocks |
|---|---|
| **Coloured metal** | Black, Silver, White, Green, Blue, Red, Copper, Light Blue, Lime, Magenta, Orange, Pink, Purple, Yellow and Iridescent Metal |
| **Concrete block** | Concrete Block, Split-Face Block, Ground-Face Block, Glazed Block |
| **Brick** | Red, Brown, Buff and Grey Brick, each with three trim blocks |
| **Stucco** | Smooth, Sand Float and Knockdown Stucco |
| **Siding** | Fiber Cement Lap, Board and Batten, Cedar Shingle and Vinyl Siding |
| **Cladding** | Corrugated Steel, Standing Seam, Insulated Panel and Composite Panel Cladding |
| **Stone veneer** | Ashlar Stone Veneer, Fieldstone Veneer, Cast Stone Veneer |

Each material comes in **one colour or finish per profile**, on purpose: siding and cladding come
in the colour each profile is most often seen in, and stucco is one warm off-white in three
finishes. The choice you make is the material, not the shade.

**Courses line up.** Brick, block, siding and cast stone are drawn so a course line falls on every
block edge and on the edge of a slab. A slab of siding ends on a board's edge, not halfway up one,
and a wall of any of them runs on across block seams with no visible repeat. Ashlar is the
exception: its courses are uneven, as dressed stone is, so a slab cuts through a stone.

### Brick trims

Each brick colour has three trim blocks, which lay into a plain wall of the same colour in bond:

- **(Soldier Course)**: a band of bricks stood on end across the middle of the block, with plain
  courses above and below. Use a row of them over windows or along the top of a wall.
- **(Header Course)**: seven plain courses over one course of brick ends, drawn darker as a
  brick's end is. Stacked, it makes common bond; in a single row it is a header band.
- **(Weep Holes)**: the plain wall with an open joint in its bottom course. Use it for the course
  just above the ground or a window head, where a real cavity wall drains.

The trims are full blocks only. They have no stairs, slab or fence.

## Glazing

Eight kinds of glass, each as a full block and as a pane:

| Glass | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Clear Glass** | |
| **Grey Tinted**, **Bronze Tinted**, **Blue Tinted Glass** | Office and storefront tints |
| **One-Way Glass** | Dark from outside, clear from inside (see below) |
| **Wired Glass** | Clear glass with a wire mesh, as in fire doors and stairwells |
| **Bullet-Resistant Glass** | Far harder to break, and as blast resistant as obsidian |
| **Frosted Glass** | Lets light through but not the view |

**Glass of one kind joins into one window.** Blocks of the same glass side by side show no seams,
and a thin dark bronze frame runs only around the outside of the whole window. Two different kinds
are two windows, so a frame runs between them.

**Panes** join like vanilla panes. A pane of the same glass carries straight on with no frame. Where
a pane meets a wall, a different pane or a vanilla pane, it is framed there, and a mullion stands
where a pane turns a corner, branches or ends in the open. Frames run along the top and bottom of
the whole window.

**One-way glass** is dark on the outside face and clear on the inside. The outside is **the way you
are looking when you place it**, so stand inside the room and face out. Panes work the same way.
From outside the dark face hides the room completely.

**A pane is a railing.** To anything walking into it, a pane is a block and a half tall, as a fence
is, so no one can jump over it and a run of panes makes a glass balustrade. You can still reach
over it to click the block beyond.

All of this glass drops itself when broken, unlike vanilla glass. It needs no silk touch.

## Structure and framing

The **Structure & Framing** tab is a building before anything closes it in: stud walls, joists and
deck, and structural steel. Nothing here is a finished surface, so a building can be shown
part-built, with its studs open and its bays visible.

### Stud walls

| Steel | Wood |
|---|---|
| Steel Stud Wall, (Narrow), (Braced) | Wood Stud Wall, (Narrow), (Braced), (Fire Blocking) |
| Steel Stud Wall (Door Opening), (Window Opening) | Wood Stud Wall (Door Opening), (Window Opening) |
| Steel Track | Wood Sole Plate |
| Hollow Metal Door Frame | |

**Walls join each other like fences.** Place stud wall blocks in a line and they make one wall.
Place one beside the line and it turns a corner, or makes a T or a cross. Any framing joins any
other: a run can go from a plain bay to a door opening to a braced bay, and from steel to wood,
without ever stopping being one wall. A wall run into a solid block does **not** join it. It ends
there and shows its end stud, as a real wall does where it meets masonry.

- **Studs run the full height.** Stacked wall blocks draw as one tall wall, with the track (or
  plate) only at the floor and at the top, not at every block.
- **A straight run has no post in the middle of each block.** A post appears only at a corner, a
  junction or the end of a run, which keeps the studs evenly spaced along the whole wall.
- **The way you face when placing** matters only for a lone block. Once a wall has neighbours, its
  connections decide which way it runs.
- **Door openings and the hollow metal door frame have no collision**, so you can walk through
  them. The **Steel Track** and **Wood Sole Plate** lie flat on the floor, a sixteenth of a block
  high, to set out where a wall will go.

**Insulation.** The plain, narrow and braced walls (and the wood Fire Blocking wall) also come with
**Batt Insulation** filled in between the studs, as a separate entry in the creative tab beside the
open wall. Pick-block on an insulated wall gives you the insulated one.

!!! note "Insulated walls are creative-only"

    The CSM Fabricator makes only the open wall. To build an insulated wall in survival, you need
    creative mode or a server operator.

### Joists, deck and rafters

**Wood Joist**, **Wood I-Joist**, **Open-Web Bar Joist**, **Joist Girder**, **Metal Roof Deck**,
**Metal Deck with Concrete** and **Ceiling Joist** each span the block in one direction, and a row
of them runs on with no break.

- They span **across the way you are looking** when you place them, which is how you lay joists in
  practice: stand in the room they cross and place them in front of you.
- Each member is only as solid as it looks, so you can walk between joists and stand on them
  before the deck goes down.

The **Wood Rafter** runs corner to corner of its block at 45 degrees. Stack rafters diagonally, the
way you stack stairs, and they make one unbroken 12:12 roof slope as long as you build it. It
faces the way you place it, so you can build both slopes of a roof up to a ridge. There are no
roof trusses: a real truss spans six to twelve blocks, which a single block can't show.

### Structural steel

**Steel Column**, **Steel Beam**, **Steel Base Plate**, **Steel Connection** and **Steel X-Brace**,
each in **(Primer)** (red oxide) and **(Galvanized)**.

Columns and beams fit together:

- A column with a beam above or below it reaches up or down to the beam's flange, so there is no
  gap.
- A beam over a column (or a Steel Connection) stops at the column with an end plate, unless
  another beam carries on in the same direction.
- A beam that ends against the side of a crossing beam reaches in to its flange.

These fits follow the neighbours, so they apply to steel already built in a world.

The X-Brace and the Wood Rafter are diagonals, so they fill their whole block to collide with.
Everything else in the tab has collision only where the member is.

## Interior finishes

The **Interior Finishes** tab holds the surfaces that go on last.

### Ceilings

**Popcorn Ceiling** and the ceiling tiles: Ceiling Tile (Standard 1, 2, 3), (Fancy), (Fancy
Dark), (Dark 1, 2, 3) and (50s 1, 2, 3). They are full blocks; build them into the ceiling.

### Window treatments

Blinds, shades and curtains: **Venetian Blind (White)**, **Roller Shade** (White, Grey and
Blackout), **Vertical Blind (White)**, **Curtain** (Beige, Grey and Navy) and the **Sheer
Curtain**.

A blind is its own thin block. **Place it against the window from the room side**: on our glass,
vanilla glass, or an open window. Placed on a floor or ceiling, it hangs facing the way you look.
It has no collision, so you can reach past it to the window.

**Blinds of one kind, hung the same way, join into one blind.** The headrail, rod or cassette runs
along the top, a bottom rail along the bottom, and a drawn curtain's fabric bunches at the two ends
of the run, however many blocks it covers.

| Blind | Right-click steps through | Light it blocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Venetian** | open, tilted, closed, raised | Some when tilted, more when closed |
| **Roller** | down, half, up | Some when down, less at half; the **Blackout** shade blocks all light down and about half at half |
| **Vertical** | open, closed, drawn aside | Some when closed |
| **Curtain** | closed, open | Some when closed; the **Sheer** one barely any |

- **Right-click changes the whole joined blind at once.** A new blind comes out closed. Sneak and
  right-click to place a block against it instead.
- **Redstone closes it, and taking the signal away opens it**, like a motorised shade. It acts only
  when the signal changes, so a blind you closed by hand is not opened by something nearby.
- A blind closed by a signal that goes away while the world is closed stays closed after you load
  it again, until you click it.

### Flooring

Three ways to floor a room:

| Kind | Blocks | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Overlay** | Carpet Tile (Grey, Blue, Charcoal), Vinyl Composition Tile (White, Beige), Ceramic Floor Tile (White, Grey), Hardwood Floor (Oak, Walnut), Polished Concrete Floor, Rubber Floor (Studded) | You want a finish on an existing floor, like vanilla carpet |
| **Full block** | Carpet Tile Block, Vinyl Composition Tile Block, Ceramic Floor Tile Block (each colour) and Rubber Floor Block (Studded) | You want the floor at full block height |
| **Set** | Polished Concrete, Oak Hardwood and Walnut Hardwood (block, stairs, slab, fence) | The finish is also the structure: stairs, landings, a raised floor |

- An **overlay** is one pixel thick and needs a solid block under it. Remove the block below and it
  pops off and drops.
- Furniture is drawn to stand on a full block, so on an overlay it sits a pixel into the floor. Use
  the **full block** versions under furniture if that bothers you.
- **Hardwood** boards run the way you are facing when you place them, so a room keeps one board
  direction.
- Floors show **no visible repeat**: carpet tiles are laid quarter-turned, and hardwood and the
  tiles vary from block to block. A big room reads as one floor, not a grid.
- Carpet, rubber and hardwood come up by hand (hardwood fastest with an axe). Tile and polished
  concrete need a pickaxe.

### Wall finishes

A thin finish hung on the face of any wall, whatever it is built of:

- **Painted Drywall** in White, Off-White, Greige, Light Grey, Pale Blue and Sage
- **Ceramic Wall Tile** (White Subway, Green Subway, White Square)
- **Acoustic Wall Panel** (Grey, Blue, Charcoal)
- **Beadboard (White)** and **Wood Slat Wall (Oak)**

**Place it against the wall.** Placed on a floor or ceiling, it hangs on the wall you are facing.

**The same finish on the same wall joins into one run**, with its trim only where it belongs: tile
gets a bullnose cap and beadboard a chair rail along the **top** of the run, both get an edge trim
at the run's ends, and an acoustic panel gets a frame round the outside of the whole panel. Paint
has no trim. So a run of tile two blocks high with paint above it is a wainscot.

- **Inside corners** just work. Where the same finish meets itself in a corner, the two runs meet
  with no trim. Where a different finish is in the corner, the run carries on into the corner and
  stops cleanly against it, as a wainscot meets the next wall.
- **Corner Guards** (Stainless and White Vinyl) cover an **outside** corner. Place one on the wall
  face beside the corner, clicking the half of the face nearest the corner: the guard wraps round
  that edge. A guard placed on top of or under another takes its position, so a stack is one tall
  guard.
- Drywall, acoustic panels, beadboard and slat wall come off by hand. Tile and the stainless guard
  need a pickaxe.
