# Construction Site

Everything that stands around a building while it goes up: scaffolding, formwork, shoring and
rebar, site fences, earthworks, the materials stacked about the site, its facilities, and a tower
crane. It's the **Construction Site** tab of the **CSM: Building Materials** module.

Most of these blocks work out how they look from the blocks around them, so you build them the
way the real thing is put together: a scaffold bay at a time, a container a block at a time, a
crane mast a section at a time.

## Frame scaffold

One block, the **Frame Scaffold**, is one bay. Stack and line them up and each bay decides its own
parts:

- an end frame on its near side, and on its far side only where the run ends
- a cross brace on each open long face
- a plank deck on top, only on the top bay of a stack
- screw jacks underneath where it stands on something that isn't scaffold
- a **guardrail** (posts, top rail, mid rail and toeboard) on every top edge you could fall from

The frames run across the way you are looking when you place a bay, so lay a run along a wall
while facing the wall and the frames come out at right angles to it, with the braces along it.

Guardrails are automatic, and there is no switch to turn one off. An edge gets no rail where the
scaffold carries on, where the block beside it has a solid top you could step out onto, or where
something solid stands against the edge at walking height — the building the scaffold is up
against. The rails are solid, even to a sneaking player.

### Climbing

The whole bay is a ladder. **Hold jump** inside it to climb, let go to slide down slowly, and
sneak to hold still. You don't take fall damage on the way down. The deck is only solid to
someone standing on it, so you never hit your head on a plank climbing up through a stack, and
**sneaking on the top deck** drops you back inside to climb down.

### Add-ons

Three items fit to a bay that's already placed. Right-click the bay with one to fit it:

| Item | What it does to the bay |
|---|---|
| **Scaffold Ladder Frame** | Ladder frames, with rungs, in place of the walk-through frames |
| **Scaffold Debris Netting** | Green netting over every open face |
| **Scaffold Casters** | Casters in place of the screw jacks where the bay stands on the ground |

A bay that already has that add-on refuses the item, and you keep it. To take one off,
**sneak-right-click the bay with an empty hand**. Each click removes one add-on, netting first,
then casters, then the ladder frame, and gives the item back. Breaking a bay drops the bay and
every add-on fitted to it.

All three add-ons have crafting table recipes. Netting, for example, is eight string round a green
dye, which makes four.

## Formwork, shoring and rebar

| Block | Placing it |
|---|---|
| **Wall Formwork** | Set out **across** your line of sight, along the wall you're facing. Runs and stacks into one form of any length and height. |
| **Column Formwork** | A single column form; it doesn't turn. |
| **Post Shore** | Stack two or three to make one shore. Each block works out its part: the outer tube, then the adjusting collar, inner tube and U-head at the top, with a base plate at the foot. |
| **Rebar Mat**, **Rebar Dowels**, **Rebar Column Cage** | Single props; they don't turn. |
| **Rebar Bundle** | Lies **along** your line of sight. |

## Fences

The fences join each other the way a vanilla fence does, with a post in the middle and a panel out
to each side they connect on.

| Fence | Tab | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary Fence** | Construction Site | Mesh panels in tube frames, 1.75 blocks tall, standing in precast feet with a clamp at each joint |
| **Temporary Fence (Privacy Screen)** | Construction Site | The same with green shade cloth behind the mesh |
| **Silt Fence** | Construction Site | Black fabric on wooden stakes, low along the edge of a site |
| **Chain-Link Fence**, **Chain-Link Fence (Black)** | Building Materials | Permanent chain-link, one block tall, that **stacks** |
| **Chain-Link Barbed Wire Top** | Building Materials | V arms and three strands of wire a side, for the top of a chain-link fence |

A fence only joins its own family: the two temporary fences join each other, and so do the two
chain-link finishes. The temporary and chain-link fences also run up to a solid block face, as a
vanilla fence does; the silt fence doesn't.

**Chain-link comes in one height and stacks instead.** Put one course on another and they share
one continuous post and mesh. The top rail and post caps are drawn only on the top course and the
tension wire only on the bottom one. Wherever a run ends, turns or meets another, you get the
heavier terminal post. Add the barbed wire top on the top course.

You can't jump the temporary and chain-link fences, just as you can't jump a vanilla fence. The
silt fence stands only three quarters of a block tall, so you can jump it.

## Earthworks

- **Trench Plate** — the steel road plate laid over an open trench. Put plates side by side and
  they read as one big plate, with the raised edge bar only round the outside.
- **Trench Box** — two steel side panels held apart by an orange spreader pipe. It runs along your
  line of sight, the way you look down a trench. Boxes end to end make a longer box, stacked they
  make a taller one (the top rail and lifting lugs move to the top course), and side by side they
  make a wider one: the panel between them goes and the spreader runs through, so you can build a
  box as wide as the trench. Only the panels are solid, so you can walk the trench between them.
- **Soil**, **Gravel** and **Sand Stockpile** — piles built up in eight layers, like snow.
  Right-click a pile with more of the same stockpile to add a layer. Once it's a full block, the
  next one goes on top as a new pile. Shape a heap by hand: full in the middle, fewer layers toward
  the edges. Breaking a pile gives back one stockpile per layer.

## Materials on site

Nine loads to make a site look busy: the **Brick**, **Concrete Block**, **Drywall** and **Bagged
Concrete Pallet**, the **Lumber Stack**, **Insulation Rolls**, the **PVC Pipe Bundle** and
**Conduit Bundle**, and the **Wire Spool**. Each lies along your line of sight when you place it.
Bundles fill their block end to end, so two laid in a line look like one long bundle.

## Containers and dumpsters, built to size

**Shipping containers** (blue, maroon, green, grey) and **roll-off dumpsters** (the same four
colours) don't come in a fixed size. You build them **block by block**, as any box of blocks you
like. Each block draws a wall only where its neighbour isn't part of the same thing, and a frame
rail only along an outside edge. A filled box of blocks then reads as one object, with no seams
inside it.

Any size works. These are the real ones:

| To build | Lay out |
|---|---|
| A twenty-foot container | 6 long × 2 wide × 2 high |
| A forty-foot container | 12 long × 2 wide × 2 high |
| A roll-off dumpster | 4 to 8 long, open on top |

- **Place every block of a container from its door end.** A container's doors go on the side that
  faces you when you place it.
- **Blocks of the same colour always join.** Two blue containers touching side by side or stacked
  become one big container. Make neighbouring containers different colours to keep them apart.
- **A dumpster is open on top.** You can climb in and stand in it, since only its walls and floor
  are solid.

## The job trailer

The job trailer is a site office you build hollow and walk into. Build it like a small building:
a floor, walls and a roof of **Job Trailer Wall** blocks, **Job Trailer Window** blocks where you
want windows, and a **Job Trailer Door** in a two-high opening.

You never choose which face is inside. A wall shows panelling on the side whose space has trailer
both above it and below it (within eight blocks), meaning the roof over the room and the floor
under it. Everywhere else it shows siding. Floors and ceilings follow the same rule. So:

- **Lay the floor and the roof.** A trailer with no floor shows siding inside until you lay one.
- Furniture, a lamp or the door inside the room don't change which side is in, so furnish it as
  you like.
- A trailer built as a solid box, with no room inside, looks like a closed trailer from outside.

The **windows** are glass you can see through, from either side. Windows stacked on windows form
one tall window, with the head and sill only at the top and bottom.

The **door** is one of the mod's two-block doors (see [Doors](doors.md) for closers and keypad
locks). Click it to open it, and it stays open while
redstone powers it. A door placed beside another hinges on the far side, so the pair meet in the
middle and open together.

## Portable toilet, gang box and washout

These three face you when you place them.

- **Portable Toilet** — it's two blocks tall. **Right-click it** and you're inside, sitting on
  the seat and facing the door. **Sneak** to get out, and you come out through the door. It holds
  one person at a time. Breaking either half takes the whole toilet and drops one.
- **Gang Box** — a safety-yellow job box with a diamond-plate lid. It's decorative.
- **Concrete Washout** — a lined steel pan of settled slurry, with its sign at the front.

## Tower crane

A tower crane is three blocks:

- **Tower Crane Mast** — a 1x1 lattice section, in yellow, red or white. Each colour is its own
  item.
- **Tower Crane Mast (2x2)** — a heavier section built from **four** blocks side by side in a
  square, in the same three colours.
- **Tower Crane Head** — goes only on top of a mast and draws everything above it: the slewing
  unit, jibs, cab, trolley and hook.

### Putting one up

1. **Stack the mast** as high as you like. The bottom section gets anchor feet. For a 2x2, lay
   each layer as a 2 × 2 square.
2. **Place the head on top.** It can only go on a mast. It takes the mast's colour, and on a 2x2
   it centres itself on the square, whichever of the four blocks you put it on.
3. **Right-click the crane with an empty hand** to set it up. Any block will do, including the
   foot of the mast, so you don't have to climb up to change it.

### The crane screen

| Setting | Range |
|---|---|
| **Model** | **Flat-top**, **Hammerhead** (adds an A-frame tower head with pendant lines to both jibs) or **Luffing jib** (a jib raked up from the slewing unit, with the hook hanging from its tip) |
| **Livery** | Yellow, red or white |
| **Jib length** | 8–40 blocks on a 1x1 mast; 16–80 on a 2x2 |
| **Slew** | 0–359°, the way the jib points |
| **Trolley** | 0–100% of the way out along the jib (not the luffing jib, which has no trolley) |
| **Hook drop** | 1–250 blocks below the jib |
| **Luff angle** | 15–80° (luffing jib only) |

Model and livery are buttons that step through their choices, and the rest are sliders. **The
crane changes as you drag**, but only on your screen. **Done** saves it for everyone, while
**Cancel** or **Escape** puts it back as it was. A 2x2 crane is twice the size all over: a wider
lattice and a bigger slewing unit as well as a longer jib.

The jib tip, the end of the counter-jib and a hammerhead's apex carry red **aviation lights**.
They flash slowly.

### Climbing the crane

To make the crane taller, use a mast section **on any block of the crane** without sneaking. A
real crane climbs the same way: the head moves up one block, with its settings, and the new
section goes in underneath it.

- The section has to match the mast: 1x1 on a 1x1, 2x2 on a 2x2.
- On a 2x2 crane a climb adds a whole layer, which takes **four** mast pieces.
- The new section takes the colour of the pieces you used, so a mast can be two colours.
- It won't climb at the build limit, if anything is in the way, or if you don't have enough pieces.
  A message tells you which. In creative it costs nothing.

**Sneak** to place a mast section as an ordinary block instead, for instance to build another mast
beside the crane.

### Going up it

The mast is a ladder: **hold jump** to climb and sneak to hold on. At the top you come out onto the
slewing deck. From there you can walk the counter-jib, up onto the winch and over the ballast,
stand on the cab roof, and walk the jib's bottom chords out to the tip. Those walkways turn with the
slew. Sneak on the deck to drop back into the mast and climb down. A luffing jib is too steep to
walk, but its deck, cab and counter-jib are not.

!!! note "Two cranes close together collide"

    Each crane's jib and counter-jib are solid to players, including those of the crane next door.
    If a neighbour's counter-jib swings over your mast, it stops you climbing out onto the deck.

## Survival

The scaffold add-ons are made at a crafting table. Everything else in the tab comes from the
**CSM Fabricator**. A stockpile layer costs one of the vanilla material it's made of: dirt, gravel
or sand. See [Survival & Crafting](survival-and-crafting.md).
