# Stairs & Railings

The Building module's Stairs & Railings tab (`tabstairsrailings`, 2026-10): stair railings, guards
and wall handrails that follow a flight up and level off across its landings, and the stairs they
run on. Everything is generated by `dev-env-utils/scripts/gen_stairs_railings.py`; the classes are
in `buildingmaterials.stairs`.

## A railing is a cell over the stairs

A railing cell is the air cell above a stair or a floor, with a railing along one or more of its
edges. **Stored**: the four edges, a bit each in metadata, so a landing's corner or U-turn is one
cell. **Read from the world**: `slope` (`RailSlope`), whether the rail climbs a flight below it.
The stairs are any `BlockStairs` the right way up whose facing runs along the railing -- vanilla's,
CSM's, another mod's -- and a stair's facing is the way it climbs:

- `mid`: over a stair, with the next stair up the flight beside the cell at its own height;
- `top`: over the top stair, the rail bending level halfway across;
- `foot`: no stair below, a stair beside it at its height climbing away, with a railing over it:
  the rail bending up halfway across to meet the flight;
- `none`: level, and every cell whose edges turn a corner.

Each is `pos` (climbing toward east or south, the positive end of the run's axis) or `neg`, so
**both sides of a flight one block wide share one value**: their edges run the same way and slope
together. The models are drawn on the north edge climbing east; turned to the south or west edge,
the model's east points the other way, so the blockstate draws the mirror model there.

**Placing**: against a wall the edge is the wall's side; on a floor or a stair, the edge nearest
the point clicked. Clicking a railing with the same railing adds the nearest edge it lacks. A
residential railing's newel stands in its `foot` cell, so start the railing on the floor in front
of the bottom step.

**Collision**: a guard along each edge as tall as a fence (it keeps a player on a landing); a wall
handrail only where its rail runs.

## The nosing line

Everything is drawn from one line, the line through the stair nosings, N(x), which a vanilla
stair's two half steps put at 45 degrees. Per shape it is three heights at x = 0, 8 and 16 px:
level (0, 0, 0), mid (-8, 0, 8), top (-8, 0, 0), foot (0, 0, 8). The handrail's top is N(x) + 15,
so a sloped cell's rail meets its neighbour's at every boundary, and a top or foot cell bends half
a cell in, where a real handrail levels off a tread's width past the last nosing. The treads under
a cell, F(x), are where posts and balusters stand: a mid or top cell has its two half steps at -8
and 0.

- **Posts** stand on the back (upper) tread of a sloped cell and mid-cell on a level one, so a run
  needs no end state at all: every cell carries its own post, and a run's last rail simply ends.
- **Rails and pickets are sheared prisms**: their ends are vertical and meet the next cell's
  exactly. On a slope a rail's vertical size is scaled by root 2 so it looks as thick as a level
  one.
- **Square balusters** are two to a tread; at 45 degrees a baluster on either half step is the
  same length.

## Drawn as OBJ

Every railing part is OBJ: a JSON element turns about one axis only, and a glass or cable infill
on a slope is a parallelogram. One uv per face, stretched, is enough for paint, steel and grain.
Glass styles draw in the translucent layer (two planes and a base shoe); cables are one cutout
plane whose texture rows follow the slope; turned balusters are two crossed cutout planes with the
silhouette, not a lathe. A vanilla multipart cannot retexture an OBJ, so each finish has its own
OBJ set and MTL (seven shapes each). Items are JSON icons made from the level cell's polygons.

**Stairs** keep vanilla's stair volumes and its blockstate rotations, but without uvlock, so the
safety nosing drawn at each tread's front stays there whichever way the stair faces. The open tread
stair draws only the treads and a 45 degree steel stringer; the residential stairs a painted body
under timber treads that overhang a straight flight's riser by a pixel.

## Measured (2026-10-25)

- **Quads a cell**: 13 to 54, 38 on average over the 189 railing models (`--report`).
- **Frame time**, dev client, world "Stairs Railings Test": a field of 192 flights carrying
  4,224 railing cells of every style, all in view, and the same field with the railings removed,
  in one session, input locked, uncapped: renderWork **0.58 / 0.57 ms with, 0.49 / 0.48 ms
  without**, so about 0.09 ms for 4,224 cells, some 21 ns a cell. Nothing about a railing runs
  per frame; it is chunk geometry.
- **Textures**: 23 sprites, about 7,000 px, in an atlas that was already 97.4% full.

## Traps

- **The slope's direction is world-relative.** `pos` is east or south whatever the edge, which is
  what lets two parallel edges share a value. A rule that maps `mid_pos` to `mid_pos` on every
  edge draws the south and west edges climbing the wrong way.
- **A corner cell is level.** Its edges cross axes; a corner on a sloped flight is not a thing.
- **The foot needs a railing over the first stair.** Without one a cell in front of a stair stays
  level, so a railing that only guards the floor beside a stair does not kink.
- **Measuring a texture change with `atlas_budget.py` after `git stash`** leaves the new,
  untracked textures in place.
