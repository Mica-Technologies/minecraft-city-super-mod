# Railroad Crossing System

The hardware of a highway-rail grade crossing: the crossbuck and its signs, the flasher mast
and the gate. Added 2026-09-13 to fill the one whole subsystem the catalogue review found
missing; the mod had the rail-priority preempt and the "tracks on left/right" warnings, but
nothing at the crossing itself.

`dev-env-utils/scripts/gen_gap_signs.py` (signs), `gen_rail_crossing.py` (hardware assets) and
`gen_rail_crossing_sounds.py` (the bell).

## Redstone, not a controller

Both hardware blocks extend `AbstractBlockRailroadCrossing`: `FACING` (four ways) plus
`POWERED`, read off adjacent redstone in `neighborChanged` and at placement, packed as
`facing | powered << 2`. A real crossing is run by its track circuit, and a detector rail is
exactly that -- so any train mod's detector, or a lever, activates the crossing with no traffic
signal controller in the loop. Interconnection with a signal's railroad **preempt** (ADVANCED
mode, `TrafficSignalPreempt`) is the natural second trigger and is not built; see the plan's
deferred list.

`isActive(world, pos)` is the one question the tile entities and the renderer ask.

## The flasher

The block is the mast **head** on a stub of pole, so it goes on top of any traffic pole. The
JSON model (`rail_crossing_flasher.json`) carries the crossbuck plate above the crossarm,
textured with the R15-1 sign's own texture so the two cannot differ, two lamp hoods with lens
plates on both faces, and the bell.

The wig-wag lives in the lens texture, the RRFB rule: `rr_lens_flash.png` is two 64 px frames
-- left lamp lit, right lamp lit -- at ten ticks a frame, 60 flashes a minute inside the
MUTCD's 35 to 65, and since Minecraft advances every sprite off one counter, every crossing in
the world flashes in step. The blockstate's `powered=true` variant points the `lens` slot at
the strip; unpowered it stays on `rr_lens_off`. The `_e` companion carries the lit lamp alone on
the same clock so the flash glows under OptiFine; the block emits no light.

`TileEntityRailroadCrossingFlasher` ticks on the server every 20 ticks and, while powered,
plays `railroad_crossing_bell` -- one second of three strikes, synthesised as an inharmonic
gong by `gen_rail_crossing_sounds.py` -- so the bell is continuous without a client-side
moving sound.

## The gate

The block is the mechanism cabinet. `TileEntityRailroadCrossingGate` holds the arm's angle
(90 up, 0 down) and walks it toward what `POWERED` asks for at 8 s down, 10 s up, keeping the
previous tick's angle for interpolation. The angle is visual state: ticked on the client,
never saved, and set straight to its target on the first tick after load so a chunk that loads
with a train present shows the gate down rather than lowering it again.

`TileEntityRailroadCrossingGateRenderer` draws the arm from the pivot on the cabinet's right
shoulder: 16 in red and white bands out to `getArmLength()` (3.5 blocks a lane plus half a
block), the counterweight behind, and three lamps -- the tip steady, the inner pair
alternating at 1 Hz -- whenever the arm is anywhere but fully up. The bands are coloured quads
on the one-pixel white texture with the block's own world light, so the arm is shaded like its
surroundings; only the lamps are full-bright. The arm reaches up to eleven blocks past the
cabinet, so the tile entity's render bounding box is grown by the arm length and it uses the
long render distance.

The arm extends to the block's **right** as you face it: place the cabinet on the right-hand
shoulder of the approach so the arm closes the approach lanes, and its twin on the far side for
the other direction.
